INJECTION-RECOVERY TEST OF THE VESPAGRAM DETECTOR

What was done
  A wave of known speed, period and amplitude is added to the keogram of a real quiet day,
  and the detector is run exactly as for the catalogue. Comparing what the detector reports
  with what was put in shows how weak a wave can be and still be found, and how accurate
  the catalogued speed, period and amplitude are.
  Wave: an equatorward sine packet under a Gaussian envelope (1.5 periods half-width), one per run,
    centred at a random time between 04 and 20 UT.
  Speeds [350, 450, 600, 800, 1100, 1400] m/s; periods [40, 55, 70] min; peak amplitudes [0.01, 0.015, 0.02, 0.03, 0.05, 0.1] TECu;
    over the full array (37.5-57.5 N) or confined to the south (37.5-50 N); 12 waves per combination.
  Quiet days: no catalogued train and daily-mean Hp30 < 1.5, spread evenly over 2015-2025.
  A wave counts as detected when a catalogued train overlaps it in time.

How to read the numbers
  Section 1: fraction of the injected waves that were detected (1.00 = all of them).
  'Recovered / true': catalogued value divided by injected value (1.000 = exact, 0.95 = 5% too low).
  '16-84%': the range that holds the central 68% of the cases, i.e. the typical scatter.
  '|error| > 20%': fraction of waves whose catalogued speed is off by more than 20%.

Limits
  Single plane waves at constant speed, on quiet days only. The wave is added after the
  arc-level detrending, so the loss of long periods in that filter is not tested here.
